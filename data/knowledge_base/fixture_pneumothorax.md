# Pneumothorax — development note

Pneumothorax is the presence of air in the pleural space, which can
cause partial or complete collapse of the affected lung. On a frontal
chest radiograph, findings may include a visible pleural line, absence
of lung markings peripheral to that line, and in tension pneumothorax,
mediastinal shift away from the affected side.

Radiology reports commonly describe pneumothorax using phrases such as
"small apical pneumothorax", "moderate right pneumothorax", or
"no pneumothorax identified". Negation is frequent because ruling out
pneumothorax is a standard part of chest radiograph interpretation.

When an image model suggests pneumothorax but the report explicitly
negates it, this is a disagreement that warrants human review. Neither
source is treated as ground truth.

This fixture is used to test the RADIANT evidence retrieval pipeline.
It is not clinical guidance.
